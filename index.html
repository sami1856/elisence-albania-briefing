Why This Journey Matters

Healthcare has never had more technology, yet it has never been more fragmented.

Across the world, people still experience healthcare through disconnected systems, isolated information, and repeated journeys that rarely reflect the complete story of an individual’s life.

I founded ELISENCE because I believe the next transformation in healthcare will not come from building another application or another artificial intelligence model.

It will come from creating trusted digital infrastructure that enables people, healthcare professionals, organisations, governments, and intelligent technologies to collaborate through one continuous, secure, explainable, and human-centred understanding of health.

Since late 2023, this vision has guided every decision we have made.

Rather than pursuing rapid growth or short-term innovation, our focus has been on building the foundations that truly matter: architecture, governance, trust, interoperability, medical intelligence, privacy, and responsible AI.

These are not features.

They are the conditions required for meaningful digital transformation.

Today, ELISENCE is entering a new phase.

Our mission is no longer simply to build technology.

It is to work alongside universities, research institutions, healthcare organisations, governments, and innovation ecosystems that share a commitment to improving healthcare through evidence, collaboration, and long-term impact.

Meaningful innovation is never created in isolation.

It grows through dialogue, shared expertise, scientific research, and partnerships built on mutual trust.

That is why our conversations with academic institutions, healthcare leaders, policymakers, and innovation communities are so important.

We do not come with every answer.

We come with a platform, a vision, and a willingness to listen.

We believe every country, every healthcare system, and every research community brings valuable knowledge that can strengthen the future we are building together.

This briefing has therefore been prepared with care, transparency, and respect for every institution and every individual we hope to meet.

It is not simply an introduction to ELISENCE.

It is an invitation to begin a conversation.

Because we believe the future of healthcare will not be shaped by any single organisation, government, or technology.

It will be built through trusted collaboration between governments, universities, researchers, healthcare professionals, innovators, industry partners, and the communities they serve.

If this document encourages meaningful dialogue, then it has already fulfilled its purpose.

We look forward to building the future of healthcare—together.

Sam Asi

ELISENCE & THE ALBANIAN DIGITAL HEALTH TRANSFORMATION

A Strategic Vision for Digital Health Infrastructure, Academic Collaboration and European Integration

1. THE EUROPEAN HEALTH CONTEXT & ALBANIA’S STRATEGIC MOMENT

EUROPEAN CONTEXT

Across Europe, healthcare is entering a new phase of digital transformation.

The European Health Data Space, expanding requirements for health-data interoperability, stronger patient access to electronic health information, responsible artificial intelligence and new frameworks for research and secondary use of health data are gradually reshaping the European digital-health landscape.

ALBANIA'S EUROPEAN TRAJECTORY

Albania enters this period from a position of active transformation rather than from zero.

The country is advancing through the European Union accession process, has opened negotiations across all 33 chapters, and is progressively aligning its institutions and regulatory frameworks with the European acquis. Albania also participates as an associated country in both Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, providing direct pathways into European research, innovation and digital-development initiatives.

HEALTH-SYSTEM MODERNISATION

Within healthcare, Albania has already invested in hospital information systems and broader health-system modernisation. World Bank-supported programmes have included Regional Hospital Information System infrastructure and improvements to health-information management across regional hospitals.

AFTER DIGITISATION

The opportunity therefore is not simply further digitisation.

It is to explore what can come after digitisation.

How can digital services become more connected?

How can health information become more longitudinal?

How can universities, hospitals, citizens and researchers participate in trusted digital-health environments?

How can future infrastructure be designed with Albania’s continuing European integration — and the evolving direction of European health-data governance — in mind?

These are the questions that make the conversation with ELISENCE relevant.

Albania does not necessarily need another isolated point solution, another standalone health application or another disconnected digital service.

There is an opportunity to explore a different layer of digital-health infrastructure: one that connects people, healthcare professionals, institutions, research communities and trusted technologies around a more continuous understanding of health.

2. ELISENCE — AN AI-POWERED DIGITAL HEALTH ECOSYSTEM

ELISENCE is not conceived as a feature company or a standalone healthcare application.

It is being built as an AI-powered Digital Health Ecosystem in which trusted experiences across healthcare, communication, medical information and everyday life can contribute to one continuous understanding of an individual’s health journey.

Its architecture spans five interconnected dimensions.

CONNECT

At the centre is a health-focused social and communication environment designed to help people remain connected — with each other, with healthcare professionals and with their own health journey.

The ecosystem includes social interaction, secure messaging, voice and video communication, stories, achievements, community experiences and ELISENCE Media.

The purpose is not simply communication.

It is continuity.

Health does not exist only during appointments, and connection should not disappear when a clinical encounter ends.

UNDERSTAND

ELISENCE is designed to transform isolated health events into longitudinal context.

A laboratory result can become part of a history rather than remaining a single report.

Blood-test information can be understood alongside previous results, medications, weight, nutrition, medical documents, clinical encounters and changes over time.

Medical Intelligence, Daily Brief and longitudinal health capabilities are intended to help transform separate health events into a progressively richer understanding of the individual.

Where appropriate evidence and validation exist, this longitudinal architecture can also support exploration of meaningful health patterns and risk signals over time.

CARE

ELISENCE connects digital-health intelligence with healthcare workflows.

The ecosystem includes GP and professional-care pathways, online appointments, prescription workflows, medication management, clinical documentation and secure communication between people and healthcare professionals.

ELISENCE is also exploring specialised healthcare-AI technologies and partnerships that may strengthen clinical documentation and communication workflows.

The objective is not to replace healthcare professionals.

It is to give people and professionals better context around each healthcare interaction.

LIVE

Health happens predominantly outside hospitals.

ELISENCE therefore extends into everyday health through Medical Journey, Body Scan, nutrition intelligence, image-assisted calorie recognition, weight management, activity-related context, achievements and other longitudinal wellbeing experiences.

These capabilities are not intended to operate as disconnected applications.

They contribute to a larger health context that develops with the individual over time.

TRUST

A connected health ecosystem can only be meaningful if trust is engineered into its foundations.

ELISENCE is therefore being developed around governed identity, consent, privacy, auditability, controlled access, data provenance and responsible AI principles.

Its architecture is designed with European data-protection and digital-health requirements in mind, while any future clinical deployment, regulatory positioning or cross-border health-data implementation would remain subject to the applicable legal, clinical, technical and regulatory requirements of the relevant jurisdiction.

MEET ELISA — A PERSONALISED HEALTH COMPANION

At the centre of ELISENCE is ELISA.

ELISA is not intended to be another general-purpose chatbot.

She is conceived as a personalised health companion whose understanding develops in the context of an individual’s continuing health journey.

Over time, ELISA can help remember what matters.

Recognise meaningful changes.

Explain complex information in more understandable language.

Help people prepare for healthcare appointments.

Celebrate progress.

Provide continuity during uncertainty.

And remain present across a health journey that may span years rather than individual conversations.

The distinction is important.

A general AI system can respond to a health question.

ELISA exists within an ecosystem designed to understand the context surrounding that question.

The ambition is therefore not simply better conversation.

It is contextual continuity.

LONGITUDINAL & POPULATION HEALTH INTELLIGENCE

The same principle extends beyond individual health events.

A single laboratory result provides information about one point in time.

A longitudinal ecosystem can place that result alongside previous laboratory history, medication, clinical conversations, appointments, nutrition, weight, body composition, medical documentation and other trusted health context.

This creates the possibility of a deeper longitudinal understanding of health.

At population level, and only within appropriate legal, ethical, research and governance frameworks, securely aggregated and de-identified information may also support research into broader health patterns.

Such environments could potentially help researchers and healthcare organisations study treatment patterns, medication experience, demographic variation, longitudinal health trends and other population-health questions.

The objective is not autonomous prediction or replacement of clinical judgement.

It is to create stronger evidence and richer context from health information that would otherwise remain isolated.

3. BUILDING BRIDGES BETWEEN ALBANIA AND EUROPE

One of the strongest opportunities we see in Albania is not simply national deployment.

It is connection.

Albania already participates in Horizon Europe and Digital Europe, while University of Medicine Tirana is actively strengthening European academic cooperation through Erasmus+, internationalisation programmes and European-funded projects.

Major Albanian healthcare institutions also have experience engaging internationally, creating an existing foundation on which deeper clinical and research partnerships could potentially be developed.

ELISENCE would like to explore whether digital-health collaboration could strengthen these existing bridges.

PATHWAY A — ALBANIAN UNIVERSITIES

Albanian universities could participate in joint digital-health research, health informatics studies, responsible-AI evaluation and European consortium development.

PATHWAY B — HEALTHCARE INSTITUTIONS

Healthcare institutions could contribute clinical expertise and, where appropriate and properly authorised, participate in controlled real-world evaluation and research environments.

PATHWAY C — EUROPEAN UNIVERSITIES AND HOSPITALS

European universities and hospitals could contribute complementary scientific, clinical, technical and methodological expertise.

PATHWAY D — TECHNOLOGY PARTNERS

Technology partners could contribute interoperability, AI, security and health-data infrastructure.

PATHWAY E — EUROPEAN PROGRAMMES

European programmes and innovation communities could provide structured pathways for multi-institution and multi-country collaboration.

The objective would not be to replace existing Albanian systems.

It would be to explore how Albanian and European capabilities could work together around new models of connected, longitudinal and responsibly governed digital health.

4. WHY ALBANIA, WHY NOW

EXISTING FOUNDATION

Albania is not beginning its digital-health journey from zero.

The country has already invested in digital public infrastructure and health-information modernisation, while simultaneously advancing through one of the most consequential stages of its European integration.

It participates directly in Horizon Europe and Digital Europe, and its academic institutions are actively developing deeper European research and educational relationships.

This convergence creates an important opportunity.

THE NEXT LAYER

The next stage of digital transformation does not necessarily require another isolated application or another standalone service.

It creates an opportunity to explore how existing digital foundations, healthcare institutions, universities, research communities and citizens can become part of a more connected, governed and intelligent health environment.

This is where the conversation with ELISENCE becomes particularly relevant.

ELISENCE has been developed as a continuously learning digital-health ecosystem in which trusted experiences across healthcare, medical information, communication and everyday health can contribute to a richer longitudinal understanding of the individual.

THE OPPORTUNITY

For Albania, the opportunity is therefore not simply to introduce another technology platform.

It is to explore whether existing national capabilities can connect with new models of longitudinal health intelligence, person-centred digital services, research collaboration and responsible artificial intelligence — while remaining grounded in appropriate governance, privacy, interoperability and clinical oversight.

Albania offers several characteristics that make this conversation particularly timely: an actively modernising health system, established European academic relationships, growing international healthcare collaboration and direct participation in European research and digital programmes.

These foundations create the possibility of something broader than market entry.

COLLABORATIVE ENVIRONMENT

We see the potential for Albania to become a collaborative environment where universities, clinicians, researchers, healthcare institutions, technology partners and European innovation networks can explore together how connected digital-health infrastructure might support people not only during individual episodes of care, but throughout the much longer periods of life that exist between those encounters.

Such collaboration could create stronger bridges between Albanian institutions and wider European research, clinical and innovation networks.

Universities could participate in joint research and consortium development.

Healthcare institutions could contribute clinical expertise and, where appropriate and properly governed, real-world evaluation environments.

Technology and research partners could contribute expertise in interoperability, medical intelligence, responsible AI, data governance and longitudinal health.

European programmes and innovation communities could provide pathways through which these collaborations develop into structured research, pilots and multi-country initiatives.

ALBANIA AS CONTRIBUTOR

In this model, Albania would not be treated simply as an end market for ELISENCE.

It could become an active contributor to the development of a new generation of connected digital-health infrastructure.

A place where ideas are researched.

Where technology is evaluated responsibly.

Where institutions collaborate across borders.

Where local expertise helps shape solutions intended for wider European relevance.

And where successful collaboration could create pathways from Albania into South-Eastern Europe and the wider European health and research ecosystem.

FINAL STRATEGIC QUESTION

Our interest is therefore exploratory, not predetermined.

We are not approaching Albania with a finished answer.

We are approaching with a developed platform, a long-term vision and a willingness to work alongside the institutions, healthcare professionals, researchers and leaders who understand the Albanian environment far better than we do.

And that leads to the question at the heart of this briefing:

Could Albania become one of the places where the next layer of European digital health is not only adopted — but researched, shaped and built?

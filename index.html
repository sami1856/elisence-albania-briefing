ELISENCE & THE ALBANIAN DIGITAL HEALTH TRANSFORMATION

A Strategic Vision for Digital Health Infrastructure, Academic Collaboration and European Integration

1. THE EUROPEAN HEALTH CONTEXT & ALBANIA’S STRATEGIC MOMENT

EUROPEAN CONTEXT

Across Europe, healthcare is entering a new phase of digital transformation.

The European Health Data Space, expanding requirements for health-data interoperability, stronger patient access to electronic health information, responsible artificial intelligence and new frameworks for research and secondary use of health data are gradually reshaping the European digital-health landscape.

ALBANIA'S EUROPEAN TRAJECTORY

Albania enters this period from a position of active transformation rather than from zero.

The country is advancing through the European Union accession process, has opened negotiations across all 33 chapters, and is progressively aligning its institutions and regulatory frameworks with the European acquis. Albania also participates as an associated country in both Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, providing direct pathways into European research, innovation and digital-development initiatives.

HEALTH-SYSTEM MODERNISATION

Within healthcare, Albania has already invested in hospital information systems and broader health-system modernisation. World Bank-supported programmes have included Regional Hospital Information System infrastructure and improvements to health-information management across regional hospitals.

AFTER DIGITISATION

The opportunity therefore is not simply further digitisation.

It is to explore what can come after digitisation.

How can digital services become more connected?

How can health information become more longitudinal?

How can universities, hospitals, citizens and researchers participate in trusted digital-health environments?

How can future infrastructure be designed with Albania’s continuing European integration — and the evolving direction of European health-data governance — in mind?

These are the questions that make the conversation with ELISENCE relevant.

Albania does not necessarily need another isolated point solution, another standalone health application or another disconnected digital service.

There is an opportunity to explore a different layer of digital-health infrastructure: one that connects people, healthcare professionals, institutions, research communities and trusted technologies around a more continuous understanding of health.

2. ELISENCE — AN AI-POWERED DIGITAL HEALTH ECOSYSTEM

ELISENCE is not conceived as a feature company or a standalone healthcare application.

It is being built as an AI-powered Digital Health Ecosystem in which trusted experiences across healthcare, communication, medical information and everyday life can contribute to one continuous understanding of an individual’s health journey.

Its architecture spans five interconnected dimensions.

CONNECT

At the centre is a health-focused social and communication environment designed to help people remain connected — with each other, with healthcare professionals and with their own health journey.

The ecosystem includes social interaction, secure messaging, voice and video communication, stories, achievements, community experiences and ELISENCE Media.

The purpose is not simply communication.

It is continuity.

Health does not exist only during appointments, and connection should not disappear when a clinical encounter ends.

UNDERSTAND

ELISENCE is designed to transform isolated health events into longitudinal context.

A laboratory result can become part of a history rather than remaining a single report.

Blood-test information can be understood alongside previous results, medications, weight, nutrition, medical documents, clinical encounters and changes over time.

Medical Intelligence, Daily Brief and longitudinal health capabilities are intended to help transform separate health events into a progressively richer understanding of the individual.

Where appropriate evidence and validation exist, this longitudinal architecture can also support exploration of meaningful health patterns and risk signals over time.

CARE

ELISENCE connects digital-health intelligence with healthcare workflows.

The ecosystem includes GP and professional-care pathways, online appointments, prescription workflows, medication management, clinical documentation and secure communication between people and healthcare professionals.

ELISENCE is also exploring specialised healthcare-AI technologies and partnerships that may strengthen clinical documentation and communication workflows.

The objective is not to replace healthcare professionals.

It is to give people and professionals better context around each healthcare interaction.

LIVE

Health happens predominantly outside hospitals.

ELISENCE therefore extends into everyday health through Medical Journey, Body Scan, nutrition intelligence, image-assisted calorie recognition, weight management, activity-related context, achievements and other longitudinal wellbeing experiences.

These capabilities are not intended to operate as disconnected applications.

They contribute to a larger health context that develops with the individual over time.

TRUST

A connected health ecosystem can only be meaningful if trust is engineered into its foundations.

ELISENCE is therefore being developed around governed identity, consent, privacy, auditability, controlled access, data provenance and responsible AI principles.

Its architecture is designed with European data-protection and digital-health requirements in mind, while any future clinical deployment, regulatory positioning or cross-border health-data implementation would remain subject to the applicable legal, clinical, technical and regulatory requirements of the relevant jurisdiction.

MEET ELISA — A PERSONALISED HEALTH COMPANION

At the centre of ELISENCE is ELISA.

ELISA is not intended to be another general-purpose chatbot.

She is conceived as a personalised health companion whose understanding develops in the context of an individual’s continuing health journey.

Over time, ELISA can help remember what matters.

Recognise meaningful changes.

Explain complex information in more understandable language.

Help people prepare for healthcare appointments.

Celebrate progress.

Provide continuity during uncertainty.

And remain present across a health journey that may span years rather than individual conversations.

The distinction is important.

A general AI system can respond to a health question.

ELISA exists within an ecosystem designed to understand the context surrounding that question.

The ambition is therefore not simply better conversation.

It is contextual continuity.

LONGITUDINAL & POPULATION HEALTH INTELLIGENCE

The same principle extends beyond individual health events.

A single laboratory result provides information about one point in time.

A longitudinal ecosystem can place that result alongside previous laboratory history, medication, clinical conversations, appointments, nutrition, weight, body composition, medical documentation and other trusted health context.

This creates the possibility of a deeper longitudinal understanding of health.

At population level, and only within appropriate legal, ethical, research and governance frameworks, securely aggregated and de-identified information may also support research into broader health patterns.

Such environments could potentially help researchers and healthcare organisations study treatment patterns, medication experience, demographic variation, longitudinal health trends and other population-health questions.

The objective is not autonomous prediction or replacement of clinical judgement.

It is to create stronger evidence and richer context from health information that would otherwise remain isolated.

3. BUILDING BRIDGES BETWEEN ALBANIA AND EUROPE

One of the strongest opportunities we see in Albania is not simply national deployment.

It is connection.

Albania already participates in Horizon Europe and Digital Europe, while University of Medicine Tirana is actively strengthening European academic cooperation through Erasmus+, internationalisation programmes and European-funded projects.

Major Albanian healthcare institutions also have experience engaging internationally, creating an existing foundation on which deeper clinical and research partnerships could potentially be developed.

ELISENCE would like to explore whether digital-health collaboration could strengthen these existing bridges.

PATHWAY A — ALBANIAN UNIVERSITIES

Albanian universities could participate in joint digital-health research, health informatics studies, responsible-AI evaluation and European consortium development.

PATHWAY B — HEALTHCARE INSTITUTIONS

Healthcare institutions could contribute clinical expertise and, where appropriate and properly authorised, participate in controlled real-world evaluation and research environments.

PATHWAY C — EUROPEAN UNIVERSITIES AND HOSPITALS

European universities and hospitals could contribute complementary scientific, clinical, technical and methodological expertise.

PATHWAY D — TECHNOLOGY PARTNERS

Technology partners could contribute interoperability, AI, security and health-data infrastructure.

PATHWAY E — EUROPEAN PROGRAMMES

European programmes and innovation communities could provide structured pathways for multi-institution and multi-country collaboration.

The objective would not be to replace existing Albanian systems.

It would be to explore how Albanian and European capabilities could work together around new models of connected, longitudinal and responsibly governed digital health.

4. WHY ALBANIA, WHY NOW

EXISTING FOUNDATION

Albania is not beginning its digital-health journey from zero.

The country has already invested in digital public infrastructure and health-information modernisation, while simultaneously advancing through one of the most consequential stages of its European integration.

It participates directly in Horizon Europe and Digital Europe, and its academic institutions are actively developing deeper European research and educational relationships.

This convergence creates an important opportunity.

THE NEXT LAYER

The next stage of digital transformation does not necessarily require another isolated application or another standalone service.

It creates an opportunity to explore how existing digital foundations, healthcare institutions, universities, research communities and citizens can become part of a more connected, governed and intelligent health environment.

This is where the conversation with ELISENCE becomes particularly relevant.

ELISENCE has been developed as a continuously learning digital-health ecosystem in which trusted experiences across healthcare, medical information, communication and everyday health can contribute to a richer longitudinal understanding of the individual.

THE OPPORTUNITY

For Albania, the opportunity is therefore not simply to introduce another technology platform.

It is to explore whether existing national capabilities can connect with new models of longitudinal health intelligence, person-centred digital services, research collaboration and responsible artificial intelligence — while remaining grounded in appropriate governance, privacy, interoperability and clinical oversight.

Albania offers several characteristics that make this conversation particularly timely: an actively modernising health system, established European academic relationships, growing international healthcare collaboration and direct participation in European research and digital programmes.

These foundations create the possibility of something broader than market entry.

COLLABORATIVE ENVIRONMENT

We see the potential for Albania to become a collaborative environment where universities, clinicians, researchers, healthcare institutions, technology partners and European innovation networks can explore together how connected digital-health infrastructure might support people not only during individual episodes of care, but throughout the much longer periods of life that exist between those encounters.

Such collaboration could create stronger bridges between Albanian institutions and wider European research, clinical and innovation networks.

Universities could participate in joint research and consortium development.

Healthcare institutions could contribute clinical expertise and, where appropriate and properly governed, real-world evaluation environments.

Technology and research partners could contribute expertise in interoperability, medical intelligence, responsible AI, data governance and longitudinal health.

European programmes and innovation communities could provide pathways through which these collaborations develop into structured research, pilots and multi-country initiatives.

ALBANIA AS CONTRIBUTOR

In this model, Albania would not be treated simply as an end market for ELISENCE.

It could become an active contributor to the development of a new generation of connected digital-health infrastructure.

A place where ideas are researched.

Where technology is evaluated responsibly.

Where institutions collaborate across borders.

Where local expertise helps shape solutions intended for wider European relevance.

And where successful collaboration could create pathways from Albania into South-Eastern Europe and the wider European health and research ecosystem.

FINAL STRATEGIC QUESTION

And that leads to the question at the heart of this briefing:

ELISENCE × ALBANIA

DIGITAL HEALTH COLLABORATION FRAMEWORK

From Shared Vision to Structured Collaboration

The opportunity we see in Albania is not defined by the deployment of a single product.

It is the possibility of bringing together technology, healthcare, academic research, public-sector expertise and European innovation networks around a structured programme of digital-health collaboration.

The previous sections of this briefing describe the vision behind ELISENCE and why Albania represents a particularly interesting environment in which to explore that vision.

The next question is more practical.

What could collaboration actually look like?

Our proposal is not to begin with a nationwide deployment, nor to arrive with a predetermined implementation model.

We believe the strongest starting point is a staged collaboration in which the right institutions are brought together, the right questions are defined, evidence is generated responsibly, and each subsequent step is taken only when the preceding one has demonstrated value.

For ELISENCE, collaboration with Albania could therefore develop through a framework connecting:

research → evidence → controlled evaluation → European collaboration → responsible scale

The purpose of this framework is to give each participant a clear role while allowing the collaboration itself to evolve through evidence rather than assumption.

01

THE COLLABORATION PRINCIPLE

Begin With Questions Worth Solving

Digital-health partnerships are strongest when they begin with real healthcare, research and system questions rather than with technology looking for somewhere to be deployed.

We therefore propose beginning by asking:

How can fragmented health information become more longitudinal and useful?

How can individuals remain meaningfully connected to their health journey between formal clinical encounters?

How can healthcare professionals gain better context without creating additional complexity or replacing clinical judgement?

How can responsible artificial intelligence support understanding, communication and continuity while remaining transparent, governed and accountable?

How can universities and healthcare institutions evaluate emerging digital-health models through credible research?

How can Albania’s growing participation in European research and innovation networks create pathways for collaborative digital-health projects with wider European relevance?

These questions should not be answered by ELISENCE alone.

They should be explored together with the institutions, researchers, clinicians, technology specialists and innovation leaders who understand the Albanian environment.

That is the collaboration we are proposing.

02

A FIVE-LAYER COLLABORATION MODEL

Rather than treating collaboration as a single pilot or funding application, we propose a five-layer model in which each layer serves a distinct purpose.

LAYER 1

EIT COMMUNITY HUB ALBANIA

The Orchestration Gateway

The EIT Community Hub Albania can play an important enabling role at the beginning of this process.

Across the EIT Regional Innovation Scheme, Community Hubs are intended to strengthen local access to the wider EIT Community, connect stakeholders with relevant Knowledge and Innovation Communities, communicate opportunities and support stronger engagement between regional innovation ecosystems and European networks. Albania is now part of the EIT RIS geography, and EIT Health activity is already visible in the country.

We therefore do not see the Hub as an organisation that should be expected to operate or own an ELISENCE project.

We see it as a potential orchestration gateway.

A gateway capable of helping identify which organisations should participate in the next conversation.

This could include introductions to:

  • EIT Health and the InnoStars ecosystem;
  • relevant Albanian universities and research groups;
  • healthcare institutions interested in digital-health research or evaluation;
  • European Digital Innovation Hubs and other digital-transformation organisations;
  • innovation agencies and ecosystem actors;
  • European programme specialists;
  • and, where appropriate, potential partners from the wider EIT Community.

The value of the Hub at this stage is therefore not endorsement.

It is connection, orientation and ecosystem intelligence.

LAYER 2

ACADEMIC & CLINICAL EVIDENCE PARTNERSHIP

From Technology to Credible Evidence

The second layer would bring together ELISENCE with academic and healthcare partners.

We believe that a meaningful digital-health collaboration should establish an evidence foundation before considering broader deployment.

A university or research institution could contribute scientific methodology, health-informatics expertise, research design, ethics and evaluation.

A healthcare institution could contribute clinical expertise, operational knowledge and — only where appropriate approvals, governance and safeguards exist — a controlled environment for real-world evaluation.

ELISENCE could contribute the digital-health platform, technical architecture, longitudinal intelligence model and product capabilities being evaluated.

Together, the partners could investigate questions including:

Longitudinal Health Intelligence

How can multiple trusted health experiences be combined into a more useful longitudinal view of the individual?

Health Informatics & Interoperability

How can information move between digital-health experiences while preserving provenance, meaning and appropriate access control?

Responsible AI

How should AI-supported health capabilities be evaluated for usefulness, transparency, safety, bias and appropriate human oversight?

Patient & Professional Experience

Can connected digital-health environments improve understanding, continuity and preparation around healthcare interactions?

Everyday Health & Prevention

How can health information generated outside formal clinical environments contribute responsibly to a better understanding of long-term health?

Clinical Workflow Support

Where can digital intelligence strengthen healthcare workflows without increasing burden or attempting to replace professional judgement?

The objective would not be to prove that every ELISENCE capability works in every environment.

The objective would be to create a credible process through which useful capabilities can be evaluated — and unsuitable assumptions can be rejected.

That distinction matters.

Evidence should determine scale.

Scale should never be used as a substitute for evidence.

LAYER 3

EIT HEALTH & INNOSTARS

Connecting the Collaboration to Europe’s Health-Innovation Network

ELISENCE is naturally aligned with the health-innovation domain of the EIT ecosystem.

EIT Health’s Regional Innovation Scheme focuses particularly on Central, Eastern and Southern European innovation ecosystems, while InnoStars programmes have included cross-border collaboration, mentoring, innovation support, market access and real-world healthcare challenges. Current programme availability varies over time, so the objective should be to identify the most appropriate live or upcoming pathway rather than assume eligibility for a particular call.

This connection is particularly relevant because EIT Health activity has already begun in Albania.

In May 2025, Albania hosted its first EIT Health Morning Health Talks, organised by EIT Health InnoStars together with Barleti Medical Group and Barleti University. The discussion brought together healthcare, academic and innovation stakeholders around digital health and the future of healthcare innovation in the region.

For ELISENCE, this suggests that the conversation does not need to begin by creating an entirely new innovation channel.

There is already an emerging one.

The practical question for the EIT Community Hub Albania could therefore be:

Which EIT Health or InnoStars pathway is most appropriate for ELISENCE at its current stage — and which people should we speak with first?

The answer may involve a programme.

It may involve a partner introduction.

It may involve a challenge, research initiative, innovation activity or future call.

The objective is not to force ELISENCE into a programme.

It is to find the programme, network or collaboration model that genuinely fits.

LAYER 4

HORIZON EUROPE CONSORTIUM TRACK

Turning Local Collaboration Into European Research

Albania is formally associated with Horizon Europe; its association agreement entered into force in May 2022 and applies from the beginning of the current programme period. This allows Albanian organisations to participate within the Horizon Europe framework subject to the conditions of individual calls.

This creates an important possibility.

A successful Albania collaboration could become the foundation for a wider European research consortium.

A future consortium might bring together:

ELISENCE

Digital-health platform, architecture, technical implementation and innovation partner.

Albanian University / Research Institution

Scientific research, health informatics, methodology, ethics and local academic coordination.

Albanian Healthcare Institution

Clinical expertise and appropriately governed evaluation environment.

European University or Hospital

Complementary scientific, clinical or methodological expertise.

Technology / Data / Cybersecurity Partners

Specialist capabilities required by the relevant project.

European Innovation Organisations

Consortium building, dissemination, ecosystem development and translation pathways where relevant.

The important point is that Horizon Europe should not be treated simply as a source of funding.

It should be used where there is a real research question that requires a European consortium to answer it.

That difference protects the scientific integrity of the collaboration.

It also makes a future proposal stronger.

LAYER 5

DIGITAL EUROPE & DIGITAL INNOVATION

From Research Evidence to Deployment Capacity

Research and deployment are different activities.

They should therefore not be forced into the same funding logic.

Where Horizon Europe is primarily suited to research and innovation, the Digital Europe Programme is focused more directly on digital capacities, deployment, uptake, AI, data, cybersecurity, digital skills and digital transformation. Current Digital Europe calls include AI/Data and related digital-capacity opportunities, with several 2026 calls currently running through 1 October 2026.

For ELISENCE, this creates a potentially complementary track.

Research partnerships may help answer:

Does this approach create useful and credible evidence?

Digital Europe or related digital-transformation mechanisms may later help answer:

How could validated digital capabilities be integrated, operationalised or scaled responsibly?

This distinction should remain clear throughout the collaboration.

Horizon Europe: research and evidence.

Digital Europe: digital capacity and deployment.

EIT Health: innovation ecosystem, validation, partnerships and market connection.

They can reinforce one another.

They should not be treated as interchangeable funding sources.

03

THE ELISENCE × ALBANIA COLLABORATION FRAMEWORK

Three Execution Tracks. One Governance Foundation.

We propose organising the collaboration through three practical tracks.

Each could progress independently.

Together, they could create a pathway from early research to broader European collaboration.

TRACK A

RESEARCH & EVIDENCE

Building the Knowledge Before Building the Scale

The first track would establish a structured research collaboration around selected aspects of connected digital health.

Potential areas could include:

  • longitudinal health intelligence;
  • health informatics;
  • responsible AI evaluation;
  • interoperability;
  • health-data provenance;
  • patient understanding;
  • professional experience;
  • digital continuity between healthcare encounters;
  • clinical communication;
  • everyday health and prevention;
  • and governed use of aggregated or de-identified information for appropriate research questions.

The first deliverable would not necessarily be a deployed system.

It could be something more important:

a shared research agenda.

From that agenda, partners could determine:

  • what should be studied;
  • what evidence already exists;
  • what requires evaluation;
  • what data would be necessary;
  • what should not be measured;
  • what governance is required;
  • and what outcomes would constitute meaningful evidence.

TRACK B

CONTROLLED REAL-WORLD EVALUATION

Evaluate Before Expanding

Where research partners and appropriate authorities consider it justified, selected capabilities could progress into controlled evaluation.

This would not mean national rollout.

It would not mean unrestricted access to health information.

It would not mean replacing existing clinical systems.

It would mean defining a limited environment in which a specific question can be evaluated responsibly.

A controlled evaluation could establish:

Purpose

What exact question are we trying to answer?

Population

Who may appropriately participate?

Scope

Which ELISENCE capabilities are included — and which are excluded?

Clinical Responsibility

Where does professional judgement remain authoritative?

Data Governance

What information may be used, for what purpose, for how long and under whose control?

Success Criteria

What evidence would justify continuing?

Stop Criteria

What evidence would require the evaluation to end?

This approach protects both participants and institutions.

It also protects ELISENCE from making assumptions that evidence may not support.

TRACK C

EUROPEAN CONSORTIUM & SCALE

From Albania to Multi-Country Collaboration

If early collaboration generates credible evidence and institutional interest, the next stage could involve a broader European consortium.

Albania could participate not merely as the location of an early pilot, but as an active research and innovation contributor.

The collaboration could then extend to complementary partners in other European countries.

Universities could expand the scientific scope.

Hospitals could contribute different clinical environments.

Technology organisations could strengthen interoperability, security and infrastructure.

Research organisations could add methodological expertise.

Innovation networks could support translation and dissemination.

And appropriate European programmes could provide mechanisms for structured multi-country collaboration.

The progression would therefore be:

local understanding → collaborative research → controlled evidence → European consortium → responsible scale

Not:

technology → deployment → hope

04

TRUST & GOVERNANCE

The Foundation Beneath Every Track

Health-data collaboration cannot begin with technology and add governance later.

Governance must be part of the architecture from the beginning.

Albania’s Law No. 124/2024 on Personal Data Protection establishes the country’s current personal-data framework and expressly aligns key elements with the EU GDPR framework. Albania’s Information and Data Protection Commissioner supervises implementation and international data-transfer requirements.

Any future ELISENCE collaboration involving personal or health information would therefore need to be designed around applicable Albanian law, relevant European requirements and the specific legal responsibilities of participating institutions.

We propose treating the following as a dedicated collaboration workstream rather than a compliance appendix:

PRIVACY

  • Purpose limitation.
  • Data minimisation.
  • Appropriate lawful basis.
  • Transparency.
  • Individual rights.
  • Retention controls.

CONSENT & PARTICIPATION

  • Clear participation models.
  • Understandable information.
  • Ability to withdraw where applicable.
  • No hidden expansion of use.

DATA PROVENANCE

  • Where did information originate?
  • Who created it?
  • When was it created?
  • Has it changed?
  • Can its history be understood?

ACCESS CONTROL

  • Who should see what?
  • Under which role?
  • For which purpose?
  • For how long?

RESEARCH GOVERNANCE

  • Ethics.
  • Protocol design.
  • Institutional approval.
  • Pseudonymisation or de-identification where appropriate.
  • Controlled research access.

RESPONSIBLE AI

  • Human oversight.
  • Explainability appropriate to use.
  • Bias evaluation.
  • Performance monitoring.
  • Clear limitations.
  • No unsupported clinical authority.

CYBERSECURITY

  • Identity.
  • Authentication.
  • Authorisation.
  • Auditability.
  • Incident response.
  • Secure data handling.

STRATEGIC COLLABORATION BRIEF

Building a Shared Case for Research, Innovation and Responsible Digital Health Collaboration

EXECUTIVE PROPOSITION

ELISENCE is exploring Albania not simply as a market for digital-health technology, but as a potential environment for structured research, institutional collaboration and European innovation.

The strategic proposition is straightforward:

Could ELISENCE and selected Albanian institutions identify a shared digital-health challenge, build credible evidence around it, and use that work as the foundation for wider European collaboration?

This proposition matters because Albania is increasingly connected to Europe’s research and digital-innovation landscape. The country is associated with Horizon Europe and with the Digital Europe Programme, while EIT’s Regional Innovation Scheme is designed specifically to strengthen innovation capacity and connect eligible regions with the wider EIT ecosystem.

For ELISENCE, this creates an opportunity to approach Albania in a way that is fundamentally different from conventional technology expansion.

Not:

Where can we deploy a product?

But:

Where can we build a credible collaboration around a problem worth solving?

The distinction is important.

The long-term value of a digital-health partnership should not be determined by the speed at which technology enters a market.

It should be determined by the quality of the questions being addressed, the credibility of the evidence generated, the strength of the institutions involved and the trust created between them.

That is the strategic case this brief sets out.

01

WHY THIS PARTNERSHIP MATTERS

Healthcare transformation increasingly depends on the ability of different institutions to work together.

Technology companies alone cannot determine what meaningful clinical innovation looks like.

Hospitals alone cannot build every layer of future digital infrastructure.

Universities cannot translate every research insight into operational technology independently.

Public institutions must balance innovation with governance, safety and long-term system interests.

Innovation organisations must connect these capabilities without allowing funding mechanisms to become substitutes for genuine purpose.

The strategic opportunity therefore exists at the intersection of these communities.

ELISENCE has been built around the idea that health information and health experiences become more valuable when they are no longer treated as isolated events.

Albanian institutions bring something equally important:

local knowledge, clinical experience, academic expertise, institutional legitimacy and understanding of the environment in which any meaningful health innovation would need to operate.

Neither side replaces the other.

The opportunity lies in combination.

This is why the proposed relationship should not be understood as a conventional supplier–customer relationship.

Its most promising form is collaborative.

02

WHY ALBANIA IS STRATEGICALLY RELEVANT

Albania is increasingly participating in European research, innovation and digital programmes.

Its Horizon Europe association agreement entered into force on 30 May 2022 and applies from 1 January 2021, placing Albanian organisations within the framework through which European research and innovation consortia are developed, subject to the conditions of individual calls.

Albania is also an associated country of the Digital Europe Programme, creating additional pathways into European initiatives focused on digital capacity, deployment, AI, data, cybersecurity and digital transformation.

The EIT Regional Innovation Scheme provides a further bridge. Its purpose is to strengthen regional innovation capacity, foster connections between local actors and improve access to the wider EIT Community.

These mechanisms matter because they create a strategic environment in which a collaboration developed locally does not necessarily remain local.

A credible Albanian research or innovation partnership could potentially connect into:

  • European universities;
  • European healthcare institutions;
  • EIT innovation networks;
  • Horizon Europe consortia;
  • Digital Europe initiatives;
  • and wider South-Eastern European collaboration.

The strategic relevance of Albania is therefore not simply geographic.

It is connective.

Albania can potentially become a place where local institutional expertise and European innovation structures meet.

03

WHY ELISENCE IS RELEVANT TO THIS MOMENT

The opportunity for ELISENCE does not come from having a long list of digital-health features.

It comes from the architecture connecting them.

ELISENCE is being developed around a longitudinal model of health in which trusted information from different experiences can contribute to a progressively richer understanding of an individual over time.

This architecture creates research questions that are increasingly relevant to modern digital health:

How should longitudinal health information be structured?

How can AI-supported capabilities remain explainable and appropriately governed?

How should data provenance be preserved as information moves between contexts?

How can professional and patient experiences become more continuous without increasing clinical burden?

How can information generated outside formal clinical encounters be incorporated responsibly?

How should different forms of digital-health evidence be evaluated before wider deployment?

These are not merely product questions.

They are scientific, clinical, technical, ethical and organisational questions.

That makes ELISENCE potentially relevant not only as a technology provider, but as a research and innovation platform around which such questions can be explored.

This distinction is central to the proposed relationship with Albania.

04

THE PARTNERSHIP THESIS

The strategic thesis behind this engagement is based on four principles.

ALBANIA SHOULD NOT BE TREATED SIMPLY AS AN END MARKET

The strongest opportunity may not be the sale or deployment of ELISENCE technology.

It may be the creation of knowledge, evidence and institutional capability together.

Albanian researchers, clinicians and organisations should therefore be positioned as contributors to the collaboration rather than recipients of a finished solution.

ELISENCE SHOULD NOT ARRIVE AS A VENDOR WITH A PREDETERMINED ANSWER

The technology already exists.

But the questions worth investigating should be shaped together.

Different healthcare systems reveal different operational realities, constraints and opportunities.

A serious collaboration should allow local expertise to influence what is evaluated and how.

RESEARCH SHOULD PRECEDE SCALE

Expansion is not evidence.

A digital-health capability should become more widely used because evidence supports that decision — not because deployment itself creates the appearance of success.

The accompanying Digital Health Collaboration Framework defines a staged execution architecture based on this principle.

This Strategic Brief does not reproduce that framework.

It provides the strategic rationale for pursuing it.

EUROPEAN COLLABORATION SHOULD EMERGE FROM SUBSTANCE

Horizon Europe, Digital Europe and EIT are valuable mechanisms.

But the partnership should not be engineered merely to fit a call.

A credible project should first establish:

  • a meaningful problem;
  • the right partners;
  • a defensible research or innovation question;
  • institutional commitment;
  • and an appropriate governance structure.

The programme should then follow the project.

Not the reverse.

05

MUTUAL STRATEGIC VALUE

A strong partnership must create value on both sides.

That value does not need to be identical.

It needs to be complementary.

FOR ALBANIAN INSTITUTIONS

A collaboration could potentially create opportunities to:

  • participate in structured digital-health research;
  • evaluate emerging longitudinal-health and responsible-AI models;
  • develop stronger links between academic, clinical and technology communities;
  • strengthen participation in European research and innovation networks;
  • develop new evidence around digital-health interoperability and patient/professional experience;
  • and contribute Albanian expertise to projects intended for wider European relevance.

These should be treated as potential outcomes, not predetermined benefits.

FOR ELISENCE

The value is equally important.

ELISENCE could gain:

  • independent academic challenge;
  • clinical and operational insight;
  • better understanding of different healthcare environments;
  • research evidence;
  • institutional feedback;
  • stronger governance design;
  • European research relationships;
  • and the opportunity to test assumptions before considering broader scale.

This is particularly valuable for a platform whose ambition extends across healthcare systems and jurisdictions.

A global architecture cannot be credibly shaped from one institutional perspective alone.

FOR EUROPEAN PARTNERS

A well-developed Albania collaboration could later provide European partners with:

  • a credible research proposition;
  • an existing cross-sector relationship;
  • an environment for comparative evaluation;
  • a technology platform capable of supporting investigation;
  • and an entry point into broader South-Eastern European collaboration.

This would remain dependent on scientific and institutional fit.

06

INSTITUTIONAL VALUE

Different organisations would participate for different reasons.

Understanding those reasons is essential.

UNIVERSITIES & RESEARCH INSTITUTIONS

The most important contribution of academic partners would not be endorsement.

It would be independence.

Universities can challenge assumptions, define research methodology, establish evidence standards and examine outcomes with scientific discipline.

A successful partnership could also support joint research development, European consortium participation and stronger links between health, informatics, AI and related disciplines.

HEALTHCARE INSTITUTIONS

Healthcare organisations provide something technology companies cannot manufacture:

operational reality.

They understand clinical workflow, professional responsibility, patient needs, institutional constraints and the difference between technically interesting ideas and clinically meaningful ones.

Their value within a collaboration would therefore be both scientific and practical.

EIT & INNOVATION ECOSYSTEM ACTORS

EIT’s Regional Innovation Scheme exists to foster connections between regional innovation actors and the wider EIT ecosystem. This makes EIT Community Hub Albania potentially valuable as an access and coordination point rather than as an owner of the project itself.

The most useful contribution may therefore be:

  • ecosystem intelligence;
  • relevant introductions;
  • orientation toward appropriate EIT communities;
  • and guidance toward credible European innovation pathways.

DIGITAL-INNOVATION ORGANISATIONS

Digital-innovation organisations may contribute capabilities that neither ELISENCE nor clinical partners should attempt to duplicate unnecessarily.

Depending on the project, this could include expertise in:

  • cybersecurity;
  • AI infrastructure;
  • interoperability;
  • data architecture;
  • digital skills;
  • deployment readiness;
  • or organisational digital transformation.

PUBLIC INSTITUTIONS

Where appropriate, public-sector participation could help ensure that projects remain aligned with wider health-system, governance and innovation priorities.

Public-sector involvement should emerge only when there is a clear institutional reason for it.

It should not be added merely to create the appearance of scale.

07

THE IMPORTANCE OF TRUST

Digital health cannot rely on innovation alone.

Health information is deeply sensitive.

The institutions involved must therefore be able to understand:

  • what information is used;
  • why it is used;
  • who may access it;
  • how long it is retained;
  • where it moves;
  • how AI contributes to decisions or explanations;
  • and who remains accountable.

Albania’s Law No. 124/2024 on the Protection of Personal Data entered into force in February 2025. The Albanian Information and Data Protection Commissioner describes the new framework as aligned with standards established by the EU GDPR, and the Commissioner is responsible for monitoring and supervising implementation of the data-protection regime.

This legal environment reinforces an important principle for any future ELISENCE collaboration:

privacy, consent, provenance, security and governance cannot be postponed until deployment.

They must influence project design from the beginning.

The accompanying Collaboration Framework sets out the operational governance architecture separately.

The strategic point is simpler:

trust is not a constraint on innovation.

It is what allows valuable innovation to survive contact with the real world.

08

WHAT SUCCESS COULD LOOK LIKE

Success should not be defined by whether ELISENCE enters Albania quickly.

A stronger definition would ask whether the collaboration produces something credible that did not exist before.

Possible indicators of strategic success could include:

A SHARED RESEARCH AGENDA

Albanian and ELISENCE partners agree on research questions worthy of investigation.

A CREDIBLE INSTITUTIONAL PARTNERSHIP

The relationship progresses beyond exploratory conversation into an agreed structure with meaningful roles.

INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE

Selected capabilities are examined through appropriate academic, technical, clinical or user-focused evaluation.

GOVERNANCE THAT CAN WITHSTAND SCRUTINY

Privacy, ethics, access, data handling and AI responsibilities are clear enough to support institutional confidence.

A EUROPEAN CONSORTIUM OPPORTUNITY

Evidence and partner alignment become strong enough to justify a multi-institution European project.

KNOWLEDGE THAT BENEFITS BOTH SIDES

Albanian institutions contribute expertise that improves the work.

ELISENCE contributes technology and architecture that enable new research and innovation questions.

REPLICABILITY — ONLY IF EARNED

If the collaboration proves useful, its methods may become relevant to institutions in other countries.

That outcome should be earned through evidence rather than assumed from the beginning.

HOW THIS CONNECTS TO EUROPE

The European dimension of this relationship matters for more than funding.

Horizon Europe exists to support research and innovation through multinational collaboration, and Albania’s association gives its eligible organisations a pathway to participate under the programme’s rules.

Digital Europe addresses a different part of the innovation cycle: building and deploying digital capacity for businesses, citizens and public administrations, including areas such as AI, data and cybersecurity. Albania is among its associated countries.

EIT adds another dimension: innovation communities and regional ecosystem connection. Its RIS framework is specifically designed to strengthen innovation capacity and connect eligible regions to the wider EIT Community.

This creates a potential progression:

credible local partnership → defensible evidence → European collaboration

But that progression is not automatic.

It should only occur when the partnership itself produces sufficient substance.

Europe should amplify a strong collaboration.

It should not be used to disguise a weak one.

STRATEGIC RISKS & BOUNDARIES

Responsible collaboration requires acknowledging what could go wrong.

TECHNOLOGY-FIRST THINKING

If the collaboration begins with a predetermined implementation rather than a meaningful problem, it risks becoming an exercise in technology adoption rather than innovation.

FUNDING-FIRST THINKING

If partners assemble only because a call is available, institutional alignment may disappear when the funding opportunity does.

EXCESSIVE SCALE TOO EARLY

National or regional ambition before evidence can create unnecessary risk and weaken trust.

UNCLEAR CLINICAL RESPONSIBILITY

Artificial intelligence must not create ambiguity about professional authority or clinical judgement.

DATA GOVERNANCE AS AN AFTERTHOUGHT

Health-data projects cannot rely on retroactive governance.

SYMBOLIC PARTNERSHIPS

Adding institutions for credibility without giving them meaningful roles weakens both the project and any future European consortium.

CLAIMS AHEAD OF EVIDENCE

ELISENCE should not ask partners to accept capability claims that have not been appropriately demonstrated.

The collaboration should strengthen the evidence base, not assume it.

These boundaries are not signs of hesitation.

They are signs of a partnership designed to survive serious institutional scrutiny.
